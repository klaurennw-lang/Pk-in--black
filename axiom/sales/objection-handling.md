# Objection Handling

The objections you'll hear, and the responses that work. Don't memorize lines — internalize the underlying answer so you can deliver it in your own words, in real time.

---

## Price objections

### "It's too expensive."

**What they actually mean:** "I don't see the ROI yet."
**Don't:** Drop price. Apologize. Defend.
**Do:** Reframe to value.

> "Let me ask it differently — what's it worth to you to add [N hours/week] back to your calendar and have a system that compounds every quarter? If we can hit your goal of [their stated KPI], what does that translate to in dollars for your business?"

If they can't answer, it's a qualification problem — they're not sure of their own ROI. Help them do the math.

### "I can hire a freelancer for [much less]."

**What they mean:** "I'm comparing you to a single human being, not an integrated system."
**Do:** Concede the point, then expand the comparison.

> "You absolutely can. A freelance editor runs $50–100/hour, a freelance scriptwriter $75–150/hour, a freelance strategist $150–300/hour. If you hire all three, plus the tooling, plus the project management to keep them in sync — that's the cost equivalent of our [Studio] tier, and you're still the integration layer.
> 
> What you're paying us for isn't the labor. It's that we've already done the integration. The voice doc, the asset library, the analytics loop, the agent stack — all already built and operating. You step in, your brand becomes a client config, and the system runs."

### "Can you reduce the price?"

**Do:** Politely no. Holding price preserves value perception across all clients.

> "No, the price is the price. I don't discount because if I did it for you, I'd have to do it for everyone, and we'd stop being able to deliver at this quality bar. What I can offer is the annual prepay — 12 months upfront gets you 2 free, which works out to 16.6% off."

### "Can we start smaller and scale up?"

**Do:** Match them to the right tier honestly.

> "Yes — that's literally what Foundation is for. It's $1,800/mo, gives you the strategy + script layer without the production pipeline. Most clients start there and upgrade to Studio within 3–6 months once they trust the work."

(But if they're asking for "Foundation but with editing too," refuse — that's à la carte and Axiom doesn't do it.)

## Trust / quality objections

### "How do I know the AI content will be good?"

**Don't:** Promise it'll always be perfect.
**Do:** Show the quality control system.

> "Two things. First, you don't have to trust the AI — you have to trust the system. Every piece passes a voice scorer and at least one human review before it ships. Nothing reaches your audience without an editor-supervisor signing off.
> 
> Second, the way we calibrate to your voice is by training the scorer on your last 100 best pieces, then refining monthly. After 60 days, the system knows your voice better than most ghostwriters would after a year.
> 
> If you'd like, I can show you a sample script in your voice before you commit. We'll do it in the discovery process at no charge."

### "I've tried AI tools before and they were generic."

**Do:** Validate the experience, then differentiate.

> "Yeah, that's because you used a tool, not a system. ChatGPT, Jasper, Copy.ai — those are general-purpose. They have no idea who you are. What we build is a per-client system: your voice, your topics, your taboos, your asset library, your audience. The output sounds like you because the system is configured to *only* sound like you."

### "What if a piece goes off-brand?"

**Do:** Show the safeguards.

> "We have three safeguards. First, the voice scorer auto-rejects anything below 85% match. Second, the human editor-supervisor reviews every piece before it ships. Third — the gut check rule: before publishing we ask 'if this went viral for the worst reason, is the brand okay?' If the answer is no, we kill it.
> 
> Our target kill rate is 5–10% of generated pieces. That means we routinely throw away work that doesn't meet the bar. That's quality control, not failure."

## Control / autonomy objections

### "I want to approve everything."

**Do:** Welcome it.

> "That's exactly how Studio and Empire work — you approve every piece before it goes live, in a single 15-minute Friday review. The system does the production work; you keep the editorial control."

### "What if I want changes mid-week?"

**Do:** Set expectations.

> "Fine — we have a 24-hour revision turnaround built into the SLA. The catch is: if you're requesting *direction* changes mid-week, it usually means our Friday review wasn't tight enough. We'd want to fix the upstream problem rather than burn revisions on every piece. So changes happen, but the goal is they get rare over time."

### "I don't want my voice cloned."

**Do:** Respect it; offer alternatives.

> "Totally fine. The voice clone is for VO if you want it; we don't use it without explicit consent and a signed clearance. If you'd rather record your own VO, we work that into the production timeline. Most Studio clients self-record A-roll anyway and only use voice clone for short B-roll narration."

## Process objections

### "Can we skip the onboarding sprint?"

**Do:** Hard no.

> "No — and I'd be doing you a disservice if I said yes. The sprint is where we build the brand voice doc, voice scorer, asset library, and agent prompts that make every downstream piece sound like you. Skip it and you get generic content that you'd cancel by month 3.
> 
> The sprint is the most important deliverable we produce. It's why we charge separately for it."

### "Can you start in less than [N] weeks?"

**Do:** Sometimes. Be honest about capacity.

> "{{Yes / No, here's why / Maybe — let me check}}. Onboarding is engineering-heavy and we don't rush it. If we have capacity, we can start the Monday after signing. If not, the next slot is [date]. I'd rather give you a clean start in 3 weeks than a scrambled one tomorrow."

### "We don't have analytics access set up yet."

**Do:** Build setup into the sprint.

> "That's fine — getting analytics access is part of week 1 of onboarding. We'll send you the access requests for each platform; you click through the OAuth. Takes about 30 minutes total."

### "Can you do this without us giving you posting access?"

**Do:** Negotiate the level.

> "We can. The trade-off is your team has to be the one publishing each batch on schedule. That's fine for Foundation, but for Studio it adds 2–4 hours of weekly work on your end and creates a delivery dependency we can't control. Most Studio+ clients give us scheduled-post access only — we can never post in real-time without your approval."

## "Wrong fit" signals — don't try to overcome

Some objections are the prospect telling you they're not a fit. Listen to them.

- "I just need someone to clip my podcast." → Refer them to a freelancer or Opus Clip.
- "I want to grow from 0 to 100K followers in 90 days." → They have unrealistic expectations; you'll churn them.
- "Can you also do my email marketing, paid ads, and SEO?" → Wrong scope; refer to a full-service agency.
- "I'll pay you 10% of the revenue you drive." → Rev-share without retainer = misaligned incentives. Politely decline.
- "I don't want to sign a contract." → Walk.
- "Can you call me at midnight if something goes wrong?" → Boundaries problem; will burn the team.

---

## The meta-rule

Every objection is information. It tells you what they're worried about. Address the worry, not just the words.

If you find yourself talking more than the prospect during objection handling, you're losing. The right move is usually a question, not a counter.

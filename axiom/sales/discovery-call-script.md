# Discovery Call Script

**Length:** 45 minutes.
**Goal:** qualify, diagnose, prescribe a tier, schedule the proposal call.
**Format:** structured but conversational. Don't read it; internalize it.

---

## Pre-call (5 min before)

- Review their last 10 posts
- Check their follower counts + engagement ratio
- Note 1 piece you genuinely liked + 1 question you have about their content
- Look for: existing team, sponsorship deals, products linked in bio

---

## Minutes 0–5 — Open

> "Thanks for making time. Real quick before we dive in — what made you book this call? What's the moment that got you here?"

Listen. Don't interject. The first 90 seconds tells you what they actually need (not what they think they need).

Take notes on:
- Pain words ("burned out," "can't keep up," "team isn't working," "stuck at X followers")
- Goal words ("scale," "launch," "monetize," "consistency")
- Constraint words ("budget," "time," "trust," "control")

---

## Minutes 5–15 — Diagnose current state

Ask in this order. Don't skip; don't reorder.

1. **"Walk me through how a piece of content gets made today, start to finish. From idea to posted."**
   - What you're listening for: where the bottleneck is. Editing? Ideation? Posting? Repurposing?
2. **"Who's involved in that process besides you? What do they own?"**
   - Mapping the existing team. Knowing who you'll be replacing or working alongside.
3. **"How many pieces a week, across all platforms?"**
   - Volume. Sets the realistic tier match.
4. **"What's working? What got you to the audience size you have today?"**
   - The voice / angle / format you must preserve.
5. **"What's not working? Where do you lose the most time, or feel like the quality drops?"**
   - The actual pain. This is what you'll fix in week one.

---

## Minutes 15–25 — Diagnose desired state

1. **"In 12 months, what's true about your content operation that isn't true today?"**
   - The vision. Their words, not yours.
2. **"What's the business behind the content? How do you make money from it?"**
   - This determines if Empire-tier community + analyst is real value or theater.
3. **"What's a number that, if it changed, would be the biggest win? Followers? Conversions? Time saved? Something else?"**
   - The KPI.
4. **"What have you tried before to fix this? What didn't work?"**
   - Surface objections to AI tools, agencies, freelancers, etc. before they're objections.

---

## Minutes 25–35 — Prescribe + frame

By now you know which tier fits. Don't pitch yet. Frame.

> "Based on what you've described, here's what I'm hearing — tell me if I have it right.
> 
> [Mirror back: their goal, the bottleneck, the constraint.]
> 
> The way we work, that maps to our [Foundation / Studio / Empire] tier.
> 
> Quick version of what that means concretely:
> - You'd send us [X]
> - You'd receive [Y]
> - The cadence is [Z]
> - Your time investment per week is about [N hours]
> 
> Does that line up with what you're trying to do?"

Wait for their reaction. If they push back on tier, listen — they may be self-qualifying down (budget) or up (ambition).

---

## Minutes 35–40 — Money + objections

Don't dance around price. State it directly.

> "The [tier] is [$X/mo] on a [3-month/6-month] minimum, with a [$Y] one-time onboarding sprint that takes [N weeks] before the retainer kicks in.
> 
> Tools you'd bring — ElevenLabs, Runway, etc. — are pass-through, runs about [$Z/mo] depending on your volume.
> 
> Annual prepay locks in a 16.6% discount and gets you 2 free months."

Then shut up. Let them respond.

Common reactions + how to handle:

| Reaction                                | What to say                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "That's more than I expected."          | "Compared to what?" Then: discuss freelancer math, agency math, or in-house math depending on answer. Don't drop price. |
| "Can we skip onboarding?"               | "No — and here's why: [explain that the brand voice doc is what makes everything else not generic]." |
| "Can you do it month-to-month?"         | "First contract is 3 months because that's how long it takes for the system to compound. After that, monthly." |
| "Can you do less than the full tier?"   | "We don't do à la carte. Here's why: [it kills delivery quality]. If [tier below] fits better, we can do that." |
| "I want to think about it."             | "Totally. What specifically do you want to think about? Often I can answer it now and save you a week." |
| "Can you reduce the price for [reason]?"| "No. The price is the price. What I can do is help you figure out if it's worth it — what's the value of [their stated KPI] moving X%?" |

---

## Minutes 40–45 — Close to next step

If they're a yes-track:

> "Here's what happens next: I'll send a proposal by [day]. It'll have the scope, contract terms, and a sprint start date. Take 24-48 hours, sign or come back with questions. Onboarding sprint starts the Monday after signing. Sound good?"

If they're a maybe:

> "Take a few days. Here's my one ask: don't just compare us on price — compare on what's actually included. I'll send you a one-page side-by-side of [tier] vs. doing this with freelancers + tools yourself. Does that help?"

If they're a no-track or wrong fit:

> "I don't think we're the right fit right now. Here's why I'd say that: [honest reason]. If [condition] changes, hit me up. In the meantime, you might look at [referral or self-serve recommendation]."

Honest disqualification builds reputation. Don't oversell.

---

## After the call (within 2 hours)

- Update CRM with notes, tier prescription, decision-track (yes/maybe/no)
- Send recap email with: what we discussed, the tier prescribed, what's next, scheduled follow-up
- If proposal coming, note proposal due date in your calendar

---

## What disqualifies a prospect (don't sign them)

- Won't do onboarding
- Won't commit minimum term
- Wants to micromanage every output (will burn margin)
- Brand is in a category Axiom won't serve (gambling, MLM, anything legally questionable)
- Refuses to give analytics access
- Pushes price below tier minimum
- Vibes are bad — trust your gut

It's much cheaper to disqualify here than to fire a client at month 4.
